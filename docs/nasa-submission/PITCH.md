## One line

We turn NASA Earth observations into evidence an investor can audit: what is changing, where, by how much, how sure we are, and what that means for a real asset. When the evidence is not enough, the product says so.

### 0 · The problem (≈30 s)

> Infrastructure in southern Peru is financed with thirty-year horizons on climate information that is fragmented, rarely tested, and almost never traceable. A risk map tells you a colour. It does not tell you what was observed, by which instrument, how strong the trend is, or what is still unknown. We built the step in between.

### 1 · Detect the trend in a real territory (≈75 s)

> We pick a territory, not a pin: the department of Moquegua, from official IGN boundaries. The platform finds the nine NASA POWER cells that cover it and analyses each one from 2001 to today.
>
> All nine cells are warming, and the evidence survives Mann–Kendall with autocorrelation correction and Benjamini–Hochberg across cells, at up to 0.42 °C per decade. 2026 so far is the third warmest of 26 years.
>
> Now the trap. NASA POWER reports a 300 mm rain day in Moquegua in 2022. A naive tool would call that a flood regime. We cross-check against a second NASA mission, GPM IMERG, whose wettest day that year was 12 mm. We keep the value, flag it as atypical, and no rainfall-extreme index stays significant. Being a detective means refusing the convenient clue.

### 2 · Translate it into a decision for an asset (≈90 s)

Use act 2A (road corridor) or act 2B (solar); both are verified. Both end on the same idea: a conditioned decision, not a verdict.

**2A · Road corridor Moquegua–Ilo**

> The same evidence now runs along an 86 km corridor split into five segments. Official layers are read per segment: flood risk and mass movements from CENEPRED, protected areas from SERNANP, archaeology from the Ministry of Culture. Zero objects does not mean zero risk, and the geological hazard service is shown as unavailable, not as clean.
>
> The multicriteria score shows its scales, weights and stability under 2,000 random weightings. Here one segment leads under every weighting, and the reason is not rain: recent rainfall is low and almost identical along the corridor. It is the terrain. NASA's elevation model gives segment 5 a slope of almost 18 degrees, against 4 to 7 elsewhere. The first study to fund is geotechnical, not hydrological.

**2B · Solar screening Moquegua vs Arequipa**

> Two candidate solar sites. The score says Arequipa 67, Moquegua 66, and Arequipa leads in only 59 % of 2,000 weightings. That is a tie, and we print the word tie.
>
> The net present value is negative in both, about minus 3 million dollars. Is it the sun? No. The break-even irradiance is 7.7 kWh/m²/day and the best year NASA ever observed there is 6.88. What moves the decision is the energy price: it would need 64.40 dollars per MWh, against the 55 assumed. Every one of those inputs is labelled as an observation, a model estimate or a user assumption.

### 3 · Leave with evidence (≈20 s)

> One click exports an executive PDF: the four challenge questions plus our fifth, sources, versions, download dates, methods, open data gaps and every assumption. Anyone can rerun it with the same inputs and the same NASA release and land on the same numbers.

### 4 · Why it matters and what is next (≈30 s)

> We do not replace engineering, environmental, archaeological, financial or legal due diligence. We tell you where to spend that budget first, and we never turn a missing layer into a zero. Next: station counter-checks with SENAMHI, LHASA landslide nowcasts, AppEEARS vegetation and water, and more territories across the Andes.
