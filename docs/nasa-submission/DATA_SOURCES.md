## NASA POWER precipitation: temporal convention and QA finding

The connector explicitly sends `time-standard=UTC` to the NASA POWER Daily Point API. Therefore its operational day boundary is **00:00 UTC**: a dated value covers 00:00–23:59 UTC. In continental Peru (UTC−05:00), that corresponds approximately to 19:00 on the preceding local date through 18:59 on the labelled local date. POWER also supports Local Solar Time (`LST`) and uses LST by default when `time-standard` is omitted; UTC and LST daily bins must not be compared as though they shared the same cutoff.

An atypical provider value was reproduced for Moquegua (`-17.193, -70.934`) on 2026-10-22 using POWER Daily API v2.10.1. The UTC request returned **14.31 mm on 2022-09-12 and 300.21 mm on 2022-09-13: 314.52 mm over the two days**. The default/LST request redistributed essentially the same total as 259.63 mm and 54.88 mm (314.51 mm). The event remains in the analysis and is flagged as atypical; it is not silently removed and requires comparison with GPM IMERG Final and, when accessible, SENAMHI stations.

The original QA comparison appeared to show that the daily cutoff changed when requesting one variable instead of several. A controlled reproduction with identical coordinates, dates and `time-standard` did **not** reproduce a parameter-count effect: single-variable and multi-variable requests returned the same values in both UTC and LST. The reproducible cause of the different allocation is the requested time standard (UTC versus LST), not the number of variables. Request composition remains recorded as an API regression watch item because the earlier response differed, but it is not treated as a proven causal mechanism.

The same reproduction confirmed a separate POWER v2.10.1 scale defect for requests that include recent dates: `PRECTOTCORR` can arrive multiplied by 24 while still declaring `mm/day` (343.44 and 7205.04 mm for those dates). The shared scale guard compares at least three common wet days with a pre-cutoff reference window and divides only when the median ratio is approximately 24; ratio 1 is preserved and any other ratio stops the analysis. It is applied both to common point extraction and K1 point/regional sampling, per cell before spatial aggregation. After that correction, the underlying 314.52 mm event remains unchanged.

K6 live validation on 24-Oct-2026 reproduced both routes without starting the application stack. The originally reported small polygon was correctly treated as `POINT` and returned 363 days, 66.14 mm total and 6.29 mm/day maximum with ratio 1.00 verified. A larger regional polygon returned 12 intersecting output cells; the provider's 16 returned grid cells all had a verified ×24 defect and were divided before weighting, producing 87.49 mm total and 5.46 mm/day maximum. `scripts/qa/k6_regional_scale.py` reproduces both checks. This finding corrects the earlier interpretation that the recent-window 150.96 mm/day value was the September 2022 anomaly: that recent value was the separate ×24 scale defect.

GPM IMERG is the designated independent satellite countercheck for rainfall extremes. NASA POWER's official source documentation describes its IMERG precipitation as daily UTC and reports approximately **3.5 months of latency for the Final product**; this lag must be respected when matching dates. The lower-latency Late product is not interchangeable with Final and must carry its own provenance if used.

## JRC PVGIS-ERA5 countercheck for temperature, solar radiation and wind

Clima and Energía request the European Commission Joint Research Centre's public PVGIS 5.3 endpoint `MRcalc` with `raddatabase=PVGIS-ERA5`, `horirrad=1`, `avtemp=1`, `startyear=2005`, `endyear=2023` and JSON output. The provider returns monthly horizontal global irradiation `H(h)_m` in kWh/m²/month and mean 2 m air temperature `T2m` in °C. The connector retains only years with all twelve months, calculates annual temperature with calendar-day weighting, and converts annual irradiation to the NASA POWER display unit kWh/m²/day by dividing the sum of the twelve monthly totals by the number of calendar days. Missing or incomplete years are omitted, never replaced with zero.

Wind is requested from the same official PVGIS 5.3 service through `seriescalc`, because it is not part of `MRcalc`. The response field `WS10m` is wind speed at 10 m in m/s and its hourly timestamps are UTC. To keep requests bounded, the connector downloads one calendar year at a time for 2005–2023, with at most six hourly requests in flight globally, and computes a yearly mean only when at least 90% of the year's expected hours are valid. A new site is allowed 45 seconds in the user-facing pipeline: unfinished years continue in a shared background task and are reported as downloading, never as zero or as a failed measurement. Completed yearly JSON responses are cached without expiry. The annual wind trend is compared with NASA POWER MERRA-2 over the same common years and with the same multiple-testing procedure used for the other ERA5 counterchecks.

ERA5 is used only as an independent reanalysis countercheck of NASA POWER's MERRA-2 temperature and CERES radiation. Trends are compared over the exact common period 2005–2023: the NASA POWER slope is recalculated on those same years, and Benjamini–Hochberg is applied between subjects separately for each source. ERA5 is sampled at each subject's representative point; for a territory, that point value is not presented as an area average. PVGIS-ERA5 has an approximate spatial resolution of 31 km and does not replace an on-site weather station or bankable solar-resource campaign.

PVGIS 5.3 is a closed historical input for this integration, so validated raw responses are cached without expiry while preserving their original UTC download timestamp and `REAL_DATA`/`CACHED_DATA` provenance. The connector retries transient 429/5xx responses twice, does not retry provider 4xx errors, and limits monthly requests to three and hourly requests to six—both below the published 30 calls/s API ceiling. On 27-Oct-2026 an independent live request for Moquegua returned 19 complete years, mean ERA5 temperature 18.026 °C and mean horizontal irradiation 6.719 kWh/m²/day; an independent pairwise-median calculation reproduced a temperature Theil–Sen slope of +0.042114 °C/year. The application therefore confirms the warming direction while showing the material 10.6 °C level difference from the coarse MERRA-2 cell; the solar-resource level agrees within approximately 4% for the evaluated southern Peru sites. For wind, the overlapping-level comparison is materially different: Moquegua averages approximately 3.88 m/s in NASA POWER MERRA-2 and 1.76 m/s in ERA5/PVGIS (about 55% lower), so neither source is presented as bankable site wind evidence.

PVGIS states that its information is free and unrestricted to use. The application nevertheless attributes **European Commission, Joint Research Centre (JRC), PVGIS 5.3 / PVGIS-ERA5**, records the access date, and preserves the provider disclaimer. Primary references verified 27-Oct-2026: [PVGIS 5.3 API](https://joint-research-centre.ec.europa.eu/photovoltaic-geographical-information-system-pvgis/using-pvgis-5/api-non-interactive-service_en), [PVGIS hourly output definitions](https://joint-research-centre.ec.europa.eu/photovoltaic-geographical-information-system-pvgis/using-pvgis-5/pvgis-5-tools/hourly-radiation_en), [PVGIS 5.3 dataset update](https://joint-research-centre.ec.europa.eu/photovoltaic-geographical-information-system-pvgis/using-pvgis-5_en), [data sources and calculation methods](https://joint-research-centre.ec.europa.eu/photovoltaic-geographical-information-system-pvgis/general-information/data-sources-calculation-methods_en), and [licence and usage conditions](https://joint-research-centre.ec.europa.eu/photovoltaic-geographical-information-system-pvgis/general-information/usage-conditions-data-protection_en).

## Copernicus DEM GLO-30 terrain countercheck

Terrain extraction now reads Copernicus DEM GLO-30 Public from the unsigned AWS Open Data bucket `s3://copernicus-dem-30m` (`https://copernicus-dem-30m.s3.amazonaws.com`). The connector derives the 1° tile identifier as `Copernicus_DSM_COG_10_{N|S}LL_00_{E|W}LLL_00_DEM`; southern Peru examples include `Copernicus_DSM_COG_10_S18_00_W071_00_DEM` and `Copernicus_DSM_COG_10_S18_00_W072_00_DEM`. It reads the first 64 KiB of the Cloud Optimized GeoTIFF and only the compressed 1024 × 1024 block containing the requested point. Header and block responses are content-addressed and cached without expiry. No full raster is silently downloaded and no missing cell is filled.

The official product specification defines GLO-30 as a **digital surface model (DSM)**, not a bare-earth terrain model: buildings, infrastructure and vegetation can contribute to the height. Its DGED GeoTIFF uses a 1 arc-second grid, 1° × 1° tiles, 32-bit floating-point elevations, WGS84-G1150 / EPSG:4326 horizontally, and EGM2008 / EPSG:3855 vertically, in metres. The application decodes the DEFLATE/predictor-3 block and calculates Horn slope over a 3 × 3 neighbourhood using latitude-adjusted east–west spacing. At a tile edge or next to nodata, elevation may remain available but slope is omitted. NASADEM uses EGM96 and a different sensor lineage; therefore small differences are expected and the pair is a screening crosscheck, not survey control.

GLO-30 and GLO-90 are described by Copernicus as worldwide products available under a free licence, while access conditions may differ between delivery services. For the public AWS copy, the registry identifies the 2021 GLO-30 Public release and notes that some country tiles can be absent. The application uses only published public tiles and applies the official adapted-data notice because it derives point elevations and slopes: **“produced using Copernicus WorldDEM-30 © DLR e.V. 2010-2014 and © Airbus Defence and Space GmbH 2014-2018 provided under COPERNICUS by the European Union and ESA; all rights reserved.”** The complete mission-specific licence remains a release-compliance verification item if derived raster data—not just point metrics—is redistributed. Primary references verified 27-Oct-2026: [Copernicus DEM collection, specification, licence and attribution](https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/explore-data/data-collections/copernicus-contributing-missions/collections-description/COP-DEM), [Copernicus DEM Product Handbook](https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/sites/default/files/media/files/2024-06/geo1988-copernicusdem-spe-002_producthandbook_i5.0.pdf), and [AWS Open Data registry](https://registry.opendata.aws/copernicus-dem/).

## AppEEARS: authenticated task contract

AppEEARS is integrated through its official HTTPS API. `POST /api/login` uses Earthdata Basic Auth, and the returned Bearer token remains only in process memory. The connector proactively renews an expiring token, retries once after HTTP 403 as required by NASA, and invokes `POST /api/logout` in a `finally` block after every submission, status, bundle or download operation. Tokens, passwords and `user_id` are removed before any provider response reaches the content-addressed cache. Downloads follow only HTTPS redirects to NASA domains or the exact CloudFront distribution observed from the official bundle API; the Bearer token is never forwarded to object storage.

## ClimateSERV CHIRPS rainfall countercheck

CHIRPS v2.0 daily is requested from the public NASA SERVIR ClimateSERV asynchronous API as `datatype=0`, using the mean over the study polygon or a 0.05° cell around a point. The connector submits **one job per year**, runs at most six jobs concurrently and retries up to three times only for transport failures or transient HTTP 429/502/503/504 responses; provider HTTP 400 is not retried. ClimateSERV has returned transient 503 responses under load. A new multi-year area can take 3–6 minutes, so the application waits 45 seconds, marks the source as downloading and continues without inventing a value; completed closed years remain in the content-addressed cache. Operational publication lag is several weeks.

UCSB Climate Hazards Center describes CHIRPS v2 as a 0.05° quasi-global (50°S–50°N) rainfall product combining infrared satellite imagery, climatology and stations, available from 1981 to near-present. CHC states that CHIRPS data is in the public domain under a Creative Commons waiver; use must still cite UCSB CHC and Funk et al. (2015), *The climate hazards infrared precipitation with stations—a new environmental record for monitoring extremes*. ClimateSERV provides access and processing; it does not change the data licence. Version 2 production is scheduled to end after December 2026, so migration to CHIRPS v3 is a tracked maintenance item.

Scientific limitation: CHIRPS smooths extreme precipitation and can report zero in hyper-arid coastal cells. Missing value `-9999`, negative values and physically impossible values are excluded, never converted to zero. Results are a countercheck alongside MERRA-2 and IMERG, not a station observation.

## NASA landslide crosschecks (`in-lhasa` / K1b-LHASA)

K1b-LHASA was closed on 27-Oct-2026 against the public NASA Earthdata GIS `Landslides` folder. The connector reads three independent products and keeps their meanings separate. Global susceptibility is a static heuristic ranking derived from terrain and conditioning factors; LHASA is a current machine-learning probability for situational awareness; COOLR is a report catalog of events that occurred. None is a parcel-scale geotechnical assessment, and neither susceptibility nor a COOLR report is interpreted as a current probability.

The susceptibility and LHASA rasters are sampled with ArcGIS `getSamples` at the representative point plus a 4×4 grid of points that fall inside the WGS84 study geometry. CENEPRED is read at exactly the same coordinates. Counts therefore describe sampled points, not area percentages. The official service metadata independently confirms EPSG:4326 and 30-arcsecond pixels; the susceptibility service extent is approximately 60°S–72°N and its published value range is 0–5. Values 1–5 map from very low to very high. Service value 0 is treated as unclassified because NASA does not define it as a susceptibility class; nodata 127 is excluded. No absent value becomes zero.

`LHASA_Hazard_Today` covers approximately 60°S–60°N and is updated twice daily. Live samples verified probabilities in the range 0–1 and nodata −9999. The service does not expose a usable raster timestamp or published alert thresholds, so the application reports only the raw probability, its order of magnitude and the UTC query/download time. `LHASA_Hazard_Tomorrow` returned identical values at five checked points and remains unused until that relationship is documented by NASA.

COOLR is queried by the study polygon. The Peru query returned 80 report points dated 1974–2020; location accuracy ranges from exact or 1 km to 250 km. Records originate in the Global Landslide Catalog and Landslide Reporter, and each record retains its source link when provided. The catalog depends on media, databases, scientific reports and citizen submissions: it is occurrence evidence but not a complete inventory, so no-record results do not prove safety.

Cross-source discoveries are deterministic: CENEPRED and NASA susceptibility agree at a point when their five-class values differ by at most one class, and agree for the study area only when at least two thirds of shared sampled points agree. COOLR reports confirm a high/very-high map signal or contradict maps that show no high sampled point, while retaining the report's spatial-accuracy caution. Live validation over Lurigancho-Chosica and Ilo completed all six NASA queries: Lurigancho matched at 7 of 9 points and had six COOLR reports from 2009–2017; Ilo matched at only 3 of 7 points, with CENEPRED higher at the other four. Cache TTLs are 30 days for susceptibility, 3 hours for LHASA and 7 days for COOLR. Provider failures remain `DATA_UNAVAILABLE` and are retried once by the baseline orchestration.

Primary references verified 27-Oct-2026: [Global Landslide Susceptibility ImageServer](https://gis.earthdata.nasa.gov/portal/rest/services/Landslides/Global_Landslide_Susceptibility/ImageServer), [LHASA Hazard Today ImageServer](https://gis.earthdata.nasa.gov/portal/rest/services/Landslides/LHASA_Hazard_Today/ImageServer), [COOLR Reports Points](https://gis.earthdata.nasa.gov/portal/rest/services/Landslides/COOLR_Reports_Points/FeatureServer/0), [NASA Earthdata data-use guidance](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/engage/open-data-services-software/data-use-policy), Stanley and Kirschbaum (2017, *Natural Hazards* 87:145–164), and Kirschbaum et al. (2015, *Geomorphology* 249:4–15).

## COES generation labels (`in-coes`)

The connector uses the official [COES generation portal](https://www.coes.org.pe/Portal/portalinformacion/generacion) and its public `ExportarGeneracion` endpoint. It requests one closed month at a time with `indicador=1` (medidores), pauses between network requests, stores each original XLSX by SHA-256, and reads only the `PRODUCCIÓN DIARIA` sheet. The provider columns are `FECHA`, `EMPRESA`, `GRUPO/CENTRAL`, `TIPO GENERACIÓN`, and `TOTAL`. The portal labels production as **MWh**, so `TOTAL` is summed directly by exact central name and month; the connector does not integrate a 15-minute MW series.

Missing plant-months are emitted with null generation and `PARTIAL` status. Raw files are not redistributed. ## NASA POWER regional spatial sampling (K1)

`POST /api/v1/spatial-sampling/power/daily` accepts a GeoJSON geometry or Feature. Features exported by the geometry panel may include `properties.kind`, `name`, and `origin`; those fields are preserved in the response. Line strings are buffered in a local azimuthal-equidistant CRS by the explicit `corridor_buffer_km`. Polygons and COD-AB territories larger than a nominal 0.5° cell use the official `/api/temporal/daily/regional` endpoint with exactly one POWER parameter per request. A polygon smaller than one nominal cell is explicitly returned as `POINT` mode at an interior representative point, rather than implying unsupported internal spatial variation.

Regional responses are deduplicated by returned cell center. Cell footprints are inferred halfway between the centers actually returned, intersected with the subject, and weighted by geodesic overlap area. The contract returns every cell series plus a daily weighted mean, weighted standard deviation, minimum, maximum and contributing-cell count. Missing cell-days are excluded and the available weights are renormalized for that date; no missing value becomes zero. The raw response is cached by request hash and retains `REAL_DATA`/`CACHED_DATA` provenance.

The live Regional endpoint returned HTTP 422 when asked for more than 366 days (`Please do not request a time period greater than 366 days`). K1 therefore partitions an inclusive long range by calendar year; each raw fragment has its own immutable cache key. Merging requires identical returned cell centers and units across fragments, rejects out-of-range dates and conflicting duplicates, and marks the combined source `REAL_DATA` when any fragment came from the provider (`CACHED_DATA` only when every fragment came from cache). A live check on 23-Oct-2026 requested 30-Dec-2023 through 2-Jan-2025 as three fragments (2 + 366 + 2 days) and returned 370 continuous daily aggregates for six intersecting cells using POWER API v2.10.1.

NASA's documentation describes regional output on a 0.5° × 0.5° grid and limits a regional request to one parameter. The live `T2M` response reflects the native MERRA-2 grid: **0.5° latitude × 0.625° longitude**. K1 therefore records the actual center spacing instead of hard-coding 0.5° in both axes.

Live checks on 23-Oct-2026, UTC daily data for 1–3 Jan 2026:

- Moquegua department (COD-AB): `REGIONAL`, 9 intersecting cells, 99.5833% inferred spatial coverage, spacing 0.5° × 0.625°, weighted T2M 13.0592 / 11.9289 / 13.5450 °C, with per-day dispersion and cell series retained.
- La Punta district, Callao (0.773019 km² simplified geometry): `POINT`, one representative point because the district is smaller than one nominal cell; T2M 19.18 / 19.02 / 19.03 °C.

These checks validate transport and aggregation, not local climate suitability. POWER remains a gridded product and does not replace station or site measurements.

## K3 real-estate pilot evidence

K3 accepts either user Polygon/MultiPolygon geometries or the pinned IGN/OCHA COD-AB Peru boundaries already catalogued above. IGN territories retain the source's CC BY-IGO attribution, 2015 creation date and the warning that they are administrative references rather than cadastral parcels. The pinned assets contain 12 MultiPolygon territories (2 departments, 3 provinces and 7 districts); K3 validates them and explicitly flattens their polygon rings for ArcGIS intersection queries. The versioned example uses three small polygons supplied as explicit `USER_ASSUMPTION` screening areas near Moquegua, Samegua and Cerro Colorado; they are neither client data nor claimed public parcels. Their ellipsoidal WGS84 areas are approximately 105,947, 105,958 and 106,407 m².

An IGN territory is accepted as an **area of analysis**, not as a development parcel. When any geometry exceeds 2 km² (200 ha), K3 keeps the NASA and territorial exposure evidence but adds a caution to that subject and to the fifth NASA answer: the conditional NPV applies the user's developable fraction and commercial assumptions to the whole geometry and therefore does not describe a project. Project evaluation requires a user-drawn or uploaded parcel polygon. With only one subject, `margin=null` and `tie=false` explicitly mean that no comparative ranking exists.

The 2018–2023 live run (`real-estate-screening-v1.0.example.json`, analysis `a9ae20e290ce6f69`) obtained cached official NASA POWER/K1 data and NASADEM `READY` for all three subjects. CENEPRED returned flood/mass-movement intersections of 1/1, 2/2 and 1/1 respectively; SERNANP and both Cultura layers returned zero intersections, which remains only a query result. INGEMMET stayed `DATA_UNAVAILABLE`. P99 precipitation was 3.7564, 3.7564 and 3.6916 mm/day.
