## Wind-energy resource inputs

### NASA POWER Hourly Point API

The wind pilot requests `WS50M` and `WS10M` together from the official `/api/temporal/hourly/point` endpoint with `community=RE`, JSON output and `time-standard=UTC`. POWER documents hourly Point access, but not an hourly Regional endpoint. The connector downloads one complete calendar year per request, runs at most four requests concurrently, retains only hours where both heights contain a finite value between 0 and 100 m/s, and omits any year with less than 90% paired coverage. Missing values such as `-999` are discarded, never converted to calm wind. The last ten complete years inside the user period determine the hourly power-curve shape; closed-year responses are cached without expiry and preserve their original download time and `REAL_DATA`/`CACHED_DATA` provenance.

The provider version is read from `header.api.version` rather than hard-coded. Claude's 30-Oct-2026 validation observed **v2.10.3**; an independent live request for Marcona (`-15.36, -75.16`) on **10-Nov-2026** observed **v2.10.4**, returned all **8,784** paired hours for leap year 2024 and produced a direct mean `WS50M` of **5.8844 m/s**. This version change is recorded as normal provider evolution, not silently described as the older version. POWER meteorology is MERRA-2 reanalysis at approximately 0.5° latitude × 0.625° longitude: it supplies temporal variability, not bankable site wind or microscale acceleration.

### Global Wind Atlas 4.0

The independent wind-level input is the **Global Wind Atlas 4.0**, owned and operated by the Technical University of Denmark (DTU) and co-developed with the World Bank Group using Energy Sector Management Assistance Program (ESMAP) funding. The official country API paths `https://globalwindatlas.info/api/gis/country/PER/wind-speed/{50|100}` redirect to `https://gwa.cdn.nazkamapps.com/country_tifs_v4/PER_wind-speed_{50|100}m.tif`. A live one-byte range request on **10-Nov-2026** returned HTTP 206 for the 100 m file and a total size of 94,155,219 bytes. The application reads only the BigTIFF header and the ZSTD-compressed block containing the requested point; validated closed blocks are cached without expiry.

The Peru rasters are floating-point 0.0025° grids (~250 m) of long-term mean wind speed at 50 m and 100 m. The pilot interpolates to hub height, then scales the hourly NASA POWER shape to that mean level. The atlas is a microscale model, not an observation, and provides neither hourly variability nor trend; a tower or LiDAR campaign and manufacturer power curve remain mandatory before investment. A live Marcona check on 10-Nov-2026 returned **9.09 m/s at 50 m** and **9.74 m/s at 100 m** with `REAL_DATA` provenance.

Global Wind Atlas data is licensed under **Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0)**. Outputs must credit: **“Technical University of Denmark (DTU). Global Wind Atlas 4.0, a free, web-based application developed, owned and operated by DTU. The Global Wind Atlas 4.0 is released in partnership with the World Bank Group, using data provided by Vortex, with funding provided by the Energy Sector Management Assistance Program (ESMAP).”** The GIS download page warns that the API is not intended for bulk download; the production connector performs point/block range reads only. Primary references verified 10-Nov-2026: [Hourly API](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/services/api/temporal/hourly/), [POWER temporal API](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/services/api/temporal/), [Global Wind Atlas GIS files](https://globalwindatlas.info/en/download/gis-files), and [Global Wind Atlas terms of use](https://globalwindatlas.info/about/TermsOfUse).

Since ADR-046 the connector reads the country file of the country where the point falls (`/country/{ISO3}/wind-speed/{50|100}`), resolved with the Natural Earth lookup described below. Live checks on 10-Nov-2026 read the Kenya and Spain files: Nairobi 3.61 m/s and Seville 4.47 m/s at hub height. A point that falls in no country polygon (open sea, small islands) has no atlas value and the model declares that it used the NASA POWER level.

## Global scope: world alerts, place search and country lookup (ADR-046)

### GDACS event list

World alerts come from the **Global Disaster Alert and Coordination System (GDACS)**, a cooperation framework of the United Nations and the European Commission. The connector calls `https://www.gdacs.org/gdacsapi/api/events/geteventlist/SEARCH` twice: orange and red alerts worldwide, and all alert levels for Peru restricted to floods, droughts, wildfires, tropical cyclones and eruptions, both over the last 60 days. A live request on **10-Nov-2026** returned GeoJSON with one point per event, `alertlevel`, `fromdate`/`todate`, `iscurrent` as the strings `"true"`/`"false"`, `severitydata.severitytext` and a report URL: 18 orange or red events worldwide and floods and a wildfire in Peru at green level.

The alert level is GDACS's own; the platform displays it and does not recompute it. An event without a point, a known type, a level or dates is discarded rather than completed. A flood published with the text `Magnitude 0` is shown without a magnitude. The point is the one GDACS publishes: for droughts and large floods it stands for the centre of the affected area. Responses are cached for one hour, with the last valid copy shown and declared if the service fails.

Reuse was verified on **11-Nov-2026** against the official [GDACS API quick start](https://www.gdacs.org/Documents/2025/GDACS_API_quickstart_v2.pdf) and [March 2025 terms and disclaimer](https://www.gdacs.org/Documents/2025/GDACS_Terms_of_use_Mar_25.pdf). GDACS states that API data are free and requests acknowledgement as **“Global Disaster Awareness and Coordination System, GDACS.”** The platform retains that credit and the event report URL. The terms also require the operational warning already shown by the product: modelled notifications can contain errors, are not guaranteed complete or timely, require validation and do not replace alerts or decisions by competent national and local authorities.

### NASA EONET v3

The second witness is NASA's **Earth Observatory Natural Event Tracker (EONET) v3**, `https://eonet.gsfc.nasa.gov/api/v3/events?status=open&days=60`. A live request on **10-Nov-2026** returned open events with one geometry per date; the connector uses the last point and the category (severe storms, wildfires, volcanoes, floods, drought, landslides, earthquakes). EONET assigns no alert level. A GDACS event carries the mark «también en NASA EONET» when EONET lists an event of the same type within 150 km (600 km for cyclones) and with dates within 10 days. Open EONET events with no GDACS counterpart are listed without a level; EONET wildfires are used only for the match, because on the day of the check 395 of 400 open events were wildfires.

Reuse conditions were checked on **11-Nov-2026** against the official [v3 documentation](https://eonet.gsfc.nasa.gov/docs/v3), [EONET description and disclaimer](https://eonet.gsfc.nasa.gov/what-is-eonet) and [NASA Science Data licence guidance](https://science.data.nasa.gov/about/license). EONET is an openly accessible NASA metadata service, but its pages do not publish a separate dataset-specific licence. The application therefore credits **NASA EONET**, preserves each event's original source URL and does not imply that NASA licenses third-party material linked by an event. EONET explicitly limits the metadata to visualization and general information: spatial and temporal extents can be approximate and are not official.

### Open-Meteo Geocoding API

Place search uses `https://geocoding-api.open-meteo.com/v1/search` (built on GeoNames). It only turns a name into coordinates to position a site; it contributes no data to any analysis. Results are cached for 30 days. Terms were verified on **11-Nov-2026**: Open-Meteo offers API data under **CC BY 4.0**, while access to its free public endpoint is limited to non-commercial use and the published quotas (600 calls/minute, 5,000/hour and 10,000/day); commercial service requires the customer endpoint or self-hosting. GeoNames independently permits commercial reuse under **CC BY 4.0** and requires credit. Displayed or redistributed search results must therefore credit **Open-Meteo** and **GeoNames**, link the licence and indicate modifications. Primary references: [Open-Meteo geocoding documentation](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/geocoding-api), [terms](https://open-meteo.com/en/terms), [licence](https://open-meteo.com/en/licence) and [GeoNames terms](https://www.geonames.org/export/).

### Natural Earth country polygons

`backend/data/world/countries_110m.geojson` is a compact copy of **Natural Earth 1:110m Admin 0 – Countries**, retrieved on 10-Nov-2026 from the project's GitHub repository, with coordinates rounded to three decimals and only the ISO codes and names kept. Natural Earth's [official terms](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/), rechecked on **11-Nov-2026**, place all distributed vector and raster versions in the **public domain** and permit modification, electronic redistribution and commercial use without permission or mandatory credit. The project nevertheless uses the recommended “Made with Natural Earth” acknowledgement. It decides which Global Wind Atlas country file to read and whether the official Peruvian layers apply to an area. At 1:110m coasts and borders are coarse: a tolerance of 0.5° is applied, and a point in no polygon returns no country instead of a guess.

### Block models of two places (ADR-047): derived grids kept in the repository

`backend/data/zones/moquegua.json` and `backend/data/zones/taal.json` are built by `scripts/zones/build_zones.py` and versioned. Each holds a 200 × 200 grid derived from two sources already described in this document, read on **11-Nov-2026** with the shared range reader:

- **Copernicus DEM GLO-30**: the elevation of the pixel nearest to each cell centre (30 m cells for Moquegua, 35 m for Taal; four 1° tiles for Taal). Stored as block heights above the lowest cell, after a declared vertical exaggeration (×2 and ×3), together with the real Horn slope in whole degrees computed before the exaggeration. Water cells are flattened to the lowest water elevation in their 5 × 5 neighbourhood.
- **ESA WorldCover 10 m 2021 v200**: the class of the pixel nearest to each cell centre, unchanged.

Unlike the rest of the platform, which reads point values on demand, these two files **redistribute a derived raster**. The full Copernicus DEM GLO-30 licence was checked on **11-Nov-2026** against Articles 3–5 of the official [COP-DEM licence PDF](https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/sites/default/files/media/files/2025-06/copernicus_contributing_mission_data_access_v2_cop_dem_licenses.pdf), rather than only the product-page summary. Article 4 expressly permits reproduction, distribution, communication to the public, adaptation, modification and combination with other data. The 200 × 200 grids are adapted and combined products—not unmodified DEM copies—so **`moquegua.json` and `taal.json` may remain in the repository and may be redistributed**. They do not need to be removed and rebuilt by every user.

That permission carries continuing conditions. This section is the legal notice for the two versioned grids, and the application repeats the adapted-data credit under «Sobre esta maqueta»:

- **Adapted-data notice:** “produced using Copernicus WorldDEM-30 © DLR e.V. 2010-2014 and © Airbus Defence and Space GmbH 2014-2018 provided under COPERNICUS by the European Union and ESA; all rights reserved.”
- **Liability notice:** “The organisations in charge of the Copernicus programme by law or by delegation do not incur any liability for any use of the Copernicus WorldDEM-30”.
- The repository and application must not state or imply that the derived grids are official Copernicus information or that DLR, Airbus, the European Union or ESA endorse them.
- Anyone granted a right to redistribute or communicate these grids must retain the applicable attribution, liability and no-endorsement obligations. WorldCover remains a separate input under CC BY 4.0 and keeps its own credit.

Each zone file carries the adapted-data notice in its `sources` list. The grid values, slope calculation, water flattening, vertical exaggeration and combination with WorldCover are project transformations and are not presented as an official Copernicus product.

The findings shown inside each model («balizas») are not new data: they are results of the platform's own modules, requested from the local API when the zone is built, and they carry the build date.

### Player mode «Detectives de la Tierra» (ADR-048): missions, terrains and third-party assets

The player mode (`/jugar`) adds no new measurement. It reuses the platform's climate screening for 56 places and wraps it in a game. What is data, what is the team's text and what is simulated:

- **Mission analyses** (`backend/data/missions/missions.json`, built by `scripts/missions/build_missions.py`): one `/projects/climate-screening` request per place, NASA POWER from 2001, with ERA5 as second source for temperature. The file stores each analysis with its own generation date. Two fields are derived by the API when the missions are served, never trusted from the file: `probable_artifact` on each atypical rainfall year (the platform's rule of ADR-036: index in mm, a satellite value for the same year, and the reanalysis at least five times above it) and `heat_support` (whether ERA5 confirms the temperature class). In the current file all atypical years carry a single contrast product, GPM IMERG; three places meet the artefact rule (Lake Poopó, Jacobabad and Mount Etna), and in 20 of the 35 places with a temperature class ERA5 does not confirm it. The game shows both facts instead of hiding them.
- **Context texts** are written by the team and are not measured by the platform. Each one links one or two external sources (`context_sources`) that were opened and read on **13-Nov-2026**; four texts were corrected to match what the sources support (Nazca, King George Island, Chosica and Merapi).
- **Mission terrains** (`backend/data/missions/terrain/*.json`, 56 files of about 260 KB, built by `scripts/missions/build_terrain.py`): a window of **Copernicus DEM GLO-30** (relief in decimetres above the lowest cell) and **ESA WorldCover 2021** (cover class) per place, 4 km at 20 m cells for towns and 8 km at about 31 m for landscapes. They are derived grids under the same terms as the block models above and carry the same two notices in their `sources` list. No file has missing elevation cells; one declares sea painted by assumption where cover is absent at sea level. Houses, streets, people and vehicles are scattered over urban cells for play: they are not real objects, and the screen says so.
- **Disaster events in the game are simulated, not data and not forecasts.** The kind of event for each place (mudflow, flood, heat, eruption or avalanche) was chosen by the team from the place's known hazards (table `HAZARDS` in the build script) and starts at a fixed time; only the path of a mudflow or avalanche follows the real relief. The alert card of the game states that the event is simulated.
- **El Niño Costero news** shown in Peruvian missions (`nino-2026.ts`) quote press and official pages with outlet, date and link. Every figure was checked against its page on 13-Nov-2026. They are news context, not platform data.

## Run-of-river hydropower discharge inputs

### Copernicus EMS GloFAS v4 via Open-Meteo

The hydro pilot requests daily `river_discharge` in m³/s from `https://flood-api.open-meteo.com/v1/flood`. Open-Meteo exposes the Copernicus Emergency Management Service **GloFAS v4** seamless product at 0.05° (approximately 5 km). The connector searches nearby coordinates under a declared river-cell selection rule, starts at 1997-01-01 for this application, validates units and dates, and caches completed historical fragments. A selected grid cell represents modelled river discharge; it is not an observed gauge, a basin water balance, an environmental-flow determination or a bankable hydrology study.

Two distinct reuse layers apply. The underlying GloFAS product must retain **Copernicus/CEMS attribution** and its applicable Copernicus licence terms. Access through Open-Meteo is also governed by the intermediary's terms: as verified on **10-Nov-2026**, the free endpoint is limited to non-commercial use and fewer than **600 calls/minute, 5,000 calls/hour, 10,000 calls/day and 300,000 calls/month**; commercial use requires an appropriate paid endpoint/API key or self-hosting. The application therefore rate-limits and caches calls and does not describe the free endpoint as suitable for a commercial deployment. ## NASA POWER precipitation: temporal convention and QA finding

The connector explicitly sends `time-standard=UTC` to the NASA POWER Daily Point API. Therefore its operational day boundary is **00:00 UTC**: a dated value covers 00:00–23:59 UTC. In continental Peru (UTC−05:00), that corresponds approximately to 19:00 on the preceding local date through 18:59 on the labelled local date. POWER also supports Local Solar Time (`LST`) and uses LST by default when `time-standard` is omitted; UTC and LST daily bins must not be compared as though they shared the same cutoff.

An atypical provider value was reproduced for Moquegua (`-17.193, -70.934`) on 2026-10-22 using POWER Daily API v2.10.1. The UTC request returned **14.31 mm on 2022-09-12 and 300.21 mm on 2022-09-13: 314.52 mm over the two days**. The default/LST request redistributed essentially the same total as 259.63 mm and 54.88 mm (314.51 mm). The event remains in the analysis and is flagged as atypical; it is not silently removed and requires comparison with GPM IMERG Final and, when accessible, SENAMHI stations.

The original QA comparison appeared to show that the daily cutoff changed when requesting one variable instead of several. A controlled reproduction with identical coordinates, dates and `time-standard` did **not** reproduce a parameter-count effect: single-variable and multi-variable requests returned the same values in both UTC and LST. The reproducible cause of the different allocation is the requested time standard (UTC versus LST), not the number of variables. Request composition remains recorded as an API regression watch item because the earlier response differed, but it is not treated as a proven causal mechanism.

The same reproduction confirmed a separate POWER v2.10.1 scale defect for requests that include recent dates: `PRECTOTCORR` can arrive multiplied by 24 while still declaring `mm/day` (343.44 and 7205.04 mm for those dates). The shared scale guard compares at least three common wet days with a pre-cutoff reference window and divides only when the median ratio is approximately 24; ratio 1 is preserved and any other ratio stops the analysis. It is applied both to common point extraction and K1 point/regional sampling, per cell before spatial aggregation. After that correction, the underlying 314.52 mm event remains unchanged.

K6 live validation on 24-Oct-2026 reproduced both routes without starting the application stack. The originally reported small polygon was correctly treated as `POINT` and returned 363 days, 66.14 mm total and 6.29 mm/day maximum with ratio 1.00 verified. A larger regional polygon returned 12 intersecting output cells; the provider's 16 returned grid cells all had a verified ×24 defect and were divided before weighting, producing 87.49 mm total and 5.46 mm/day maximum. `scripts/qa/k6_regional_scale.py` reproduces both checks. This finding corrects the earlier interpretation that the recent-window 150.96 mm/day value was the September 2022 anomaly: that recent value was the separate ×24 scale defect.

GPM IMERG is the designated independent satellite countercheck for rainfall extremes. NASA POWER's official source documentation describes its IMERG precipitation as daily UTC and reports approximately **3.5 months of latency for the Final product**; this lag must be respected when matching dates. The lower-latency Late product is not interchangeable with Final and must carry its own provenance if used.

Verification sources (accessed 2026-10-22): [POWER Daily API](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/services/api/temporal/daily/), [POWER temporal processing](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/methodology/data/processing/), [POWER data sources and IMERG latency](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/methodology/data/sources/), and [GPM IMERG](https://gpm.nasa.gov/data/imerg).

## JRC PVGIS-ERA5 countercheck for temperature, solar radiation and wind

Clima and Energía request the European Commission Joint Research Centre's public PVGIS 5.3 endpoint `MRcalc` with `raddatabase=PVGIS-ERA5`, `horirrad=1`, `avtemp=1`, `startyear=2005`, `endyear=2023` and JSON output. The provider returns monthly horizontal global irradiation `H(h)_m` in kWh/m²/month and mean 2 m air temperature `T2m` in °C. The connector retains only years with all twelve months, calculates annual temperature with calendar-day weighting, and converts annual irradiation to the NASA POWER display unit kWh/m²/day by dividing the sum of the twelve monthly totals by the number of calendar days. Missing or incomplete years are omitted, never replaced with zero.

Wind is requested from the same official PVGIS 5.3 service through `seriescalc`, because it is not part of `MRcalc`. The response field `WS10m` is wind speed at 10 m in m/s and its hourly timestamps are UTC. To keep requests bounded, the connector downloads one calendar year at a time for 2005–2023, with at most six hourly requests in flight globally, and computes a yearly mean only when at least 90% of the year's expected hours are valid. A new site is allowed 45 seconds in the user-facing pipeline: unfinished years continue in a shared background task and are reported as downloading, never as zero or as a failed measurement. Completed yearly JSON responses are cached without expiry. The annual wind trend is compared with NASA POWER MERRA-2 over the same common years and with the same multiple-testing procedure used for the other ERA5 counterchecks.

ERA5 is used only as an independent reanalysis countercheck of NASA POWER's MERRA-2 temperature and CERES radiation. Trends are compared over the exact common period 2005–2023: the NASA POWER slope is recalculated on those same years, and Benjamini–Hochberg is applied between subjects separately for each source. ERA5 is sampled at each subject's representative point; for a territory, that point value is not presented as an area average. PVGIS-ERA5 has an approximate spatial resolution of 31 km and does not replace an on-site weather station or bankable solar-resource campaign.

PVGIS 5.3 is a closed historical input for this integration, so validated raw responses are cached without expiry while preserving their original UTC download timestamp and `REAL_DATA`/`CACHED_DATA` provenance. The connector retries transient 429/5xx responses twice, does not retry provider 4xx errors, and limits monthly requests to three and hourly requests to six—both below the published 30 calls/s API ceiling. On 27-Oct-2026 an independent live request for Moquegua returned 19 complete years, mean ERA5 temperature 18.026 °C and mean horizontal irradiation 6.719 kWh/m²/day; an independent pairwise-median calculation reproduced a temperature Theil–Sen slope of +0.042114 °C/year. The application therefore confirms the warming direction while showing the material 10.6 °C level difference from the coarse MERRA-2 cell; the solar-resource level agrees within approximately 4% for the evaluated southern Peru sites. For wind, the overlapping-level comparison is materially different: Moquegua averages approximately 3.88 m/s in NASA POWER MERRA-2 and 1.76 m/s in ERA5/PVGIS (about 55% lower), so neither source is presented as bankable site wind evidence.

PVGIS states that its information is free and unrestricted to use. The application nevertheless attributes **European Commission, Joint Research Centre (JRC), PVGIS 5.3 / PVGIS-ERA5**, records the access date, and preserves the provider disclaimer. Primary references verified 27-Oct-2026: [PVGIS 5.3 API](https://joint-research-centre.ec.europa.eu/photovoltaic-geographical-information-system-pvgis/using-pvgis-5/api-non-interactive-service_en), [PVGIS hourly output definitions](https://joint-research-centre.ec.europa.eu/photovoltaic-geographical-information-system-pvgis/using-pvgis-5/pvgis-5-tools/hourly-radiation_en), [PVGIS 5.3 dataset update](https://joint-research-centre.ec.europa.eu/photovoltaic-geographical-information-system-pvgis/using-pvgis-5_en), [data sources and calculation methods](https://joint-research-centre.ec.europa.eu/photovoltaic-geographical-information-system-pvgis/general-information/data-sources-calculation-methods_en), and [licence and usage conditions](https://joint-research-centre.ec.europa.eu/photovoltaic-geographical-information-system-pvgis/general-information/usage-conditions-data-protection_en).

## Copernicus DEM GLO-30 terrain countercheck

Terrain extraction now reads Copernicus DEM GLO-30 Public from the unsigned AWS Open Data bucket `s3://copernicus-dem-30m` (`https://copernicus-dem-30m.s3.amazonaws.com`). The official product specification defines GLO-30 as a **digital surface model (DSM)**, not a bare-earth terrain model: buildings, infrastructure and vegetation can contribute to the height. At a tile edge or next to nodata, elevation may remain available but slope is omitted. GLO-30 and GLO-90 are described by Copernicus as worldwide products available under a free licence, while access conditions may differ between delivery services. For the public AWS copy, the registry identifies the 2021 GLO-30 Public release and notes that some country tiles can be absent. The licence was rechecked on **11-Nov-2026**. ## ESA WorldCover 2021 land-cover countercheck

The environmental baseline reads the official **ESA WorldCover 10 m 2021 v200** map from the public AWS bucket `s3://esa-worldcover` in `eu-central-1`. Map objects follow `v200/2021/map/ESA_WorldCover_10m_2021_v200_{N|S}LL{E|W}LLL_Map.tif`; every name uses the south-west corner of a 3° × 3° tile. The files are Cloud Optimized GeoTIFFs in EPSG:4326. The shared range reader downloads the header and only the DEFLATE-compressed blocks needed for the study area, then chooses the most detailed overview whose window fits within 16 blocks per source tile. Closed headers and blocks are cached without expiry with their original download timestamp and `REAL_DATA`/`CACHED_DATA` provenance.

The uint8 map uses the official classes: 10 tree cover, 20 shrubland, 30 grassland, 40 cropland, 50 built-up, 60 bare/sparse vegetation, 70 snow and ice, 80 permanent water, 90 herbaceous wetland, 95 mangroves and 100 moss and lichen; **0 is nodata and is never counted as a land-cover class**. Composition counts pixel centres strictly inside the valid study geometry, prevents duplicate counting at exact north/east tile boundaries and samples regularly only if the selected overview would otherwise exceed 250,000 centres. The reported effective resolution includes both overview and sampling stride. The MODIS comparison uses the same ±km analysis window constructed in a local azimuthal-equidistant CRS, rather than converting kilometres to degrees.

For comparison with MOD44B 2021, WorldCover classes are grouped as trees (10, 95), non-tree vegetation (20, 30, 40, 90, 100) and non-vegetated cover (50, 60, 70); permanent water (80) is excluded from both the numerator and denominator. The result is a scientific countercheck, not equality of measurements: WorldCover assigns one dominant class per 10 m pixel, whereas MOD44B estimates fractional cover within a 250 m pixel. Agreement is therefore more informative in densely covered or clearly bare areas than in sparse vegetation, urban mosaics or lomas. The official validation reports 76.7% global overall accuracy for v200, which does not imply the same local accuracy in southern Peru. Only 2020 v100 and 2021 v200 exist, and ESA warns that their algorithm versions differ; the application uses 2021 as a level check and does not infer change between those two maps.

Outputs use the provider's required map credit: **“© ESA WorldCover project 2021 / Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data (2021) processed by ESA WorldCover consortium.”** Dataset citation: Zanaga, D., Van De Kerchove, R., Daems, D., De Keersmaecker, W., Brockmann, C., Kirches, G., Wevers, J., Cartus, O., Santoro, M., Fritz, S., Lesiv, M., Herold, M., Tsendbazar, N.-E., Xu, P., Ramoino, F., & Arino, O. ## NASA landslide crosschecks (`in-lhasa` / K1b-LHASA)

K1b-LHASA was closed on 27-Oct-2026 against the public NASA Earthdata GIS `Landslides` folder. The connector reads three independent products and keeps their meanings separate. Global susceptibility is a static heuristic ranking derived from terrain and conditioning factors; LHASA is a current machine-learning probability for situational awareness; COOLR is a report catalog of events that occurred. None is a parcel-scale geotechnical assessment, and neither susceptibility nor a COOLR report is interpreted as a current probability.

The susceptibility and LHASA rasters are sampled with ArcGIS `getSamples` at the representative point plus a 4×4 grid of points that fall inside the WGS84 study geometry. CENEPRED is read at exactly the same coordinates. Counts therefore describe sampled points, not area percentages. The official service metadata independently confirms EPSG:4326 and 30-arcsecond pixels; the susceptibility service extent is approximately 60°S–72°N and its published value range is 0–5. Values 1–5 map from very low to very high. Service value 0 is treated as unclassified because NASA does not define it as a susceptibility class; nodata 127 is excluded. No absent value becomes zero.

`LHASA_Hazard_Today` covers approximately 60°S–60°N and is updated twice daily. Live samples verified probabilities in the range 0–1 and nodata −9999. The service does not expose a usable raster timestamp or published alert thresholds, so the application reports only the raw probability, its order of magnitude and the UTC query/download time. `LHASA_Hazard_Tomorrow` returned identical values at five checked points and remains unused until that relationship is documented by NASA.

COOLR is queried by the study polygon. The Peru query returned 80 report points dated 1974–2020; location accuracy ranges from exact or 1 km to 250 km. Records originate in the Global Landslide Catalog and Landslide Reporter, and each record retains its source link when provided. The catalog depends on media, databases, scientific reports and citizen submissions: it is occurrence evidence but not a complete inventory, so no-record results do not prove safety.

Cross-source discoveries are deterministic: CENEPRED and NASA susceptibility agree at a point when their five-class values differ by at most one class, and agree for the study area only when at least two thirds of shared sampled points agree. COOLR reports confirm a high/very-high map signal or contradict maps that show no high sampled point, while retaining the report's spatial-accuracy caution. Live validation over Lurigancho-Chosica and Ilo completed all six NASA queries: Lurigancho matched at 7 of 9 points and had six COOLR reports from 2009–2017; Ilo matched at only 3 of 7 points, with CENEPRED higher at the other four. Cache TTLs are 30 days for susceptibility, 3 hours for LHASA and 7 days for COOLR. Provider failures remain `DATA_UNAVAILABLE` and are retried once by the baseline orchestration.

Primary references verified 27-Oct-2026: [Global Landslide Susceptibility ImageServer](https://gis.earthdata.nasa.gov/portal/rest/services/Landslides/Global_Landslide_Susceptibility/ImageServer), [LHASA Hazard Today ImageServer](https://gis.earthdata.nasa.gov/portal/rest/services/Landslides/LHASA_Hazard_Today/ImageServer), [COOLR Reports Points](https://gis.earthdata.nasa.gov/portal/rest/services/Landslides/COOLR_Reports_Points/FeatureServer/0), [NASA Earthdata data-use guidance](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/engage/open-data-services-software/data-use-policy), Stanley and Kirschbaum (2017, *Natural Hazards* 87:145–164), and Kirschbaum et al. (2015, *Geomorphology* 249:4–15).

## COES generation labels (`in-coes`)

The connector uses the official [COES generation portal](https://www.coes.org.pe/Portal/portalinformacion/generacion) and its public `ExportarGeneracion` endpoint. It requests one closed month at a time with `indicador=1` (medidores), pauses between network requests, stores each original XLSX by SHA-256, and reads only the `PRODUCCIÓN DIARIA` sheet. The provider columns are `FECHA`, `EMPRESA`, `GRUPO/CENTRAL`, `TIPO GENERACIÓN`, and `TOTAL`. The portal labels production as **MWh**, so `TOTAL` is summed directly by exact central name and month; the connector does not integrate a 15-minute MW series.

Missing plant-months are emitted with null generation and `PARTIAL` status. Raw files are not redistributed. ## NASA POWER regional spatial sampling (K1)

`POST /api/v1/spatial-sampling/power/daily` accepts a GeoJSON geometry or Feature. Features exported by the geometry panel may include `properties.kind`, `name`, and `origin`; those fields are preserved in the response. Line strings are buffered in a local azimuthal-equidistant CRS by the explicit `corridor_buffer_km`. Polygons and COD-AB territories larger than a nominal 0.5° cell use the official `/api/temporal/daily/regional` endpoint with exactly one POWER parameter per request. A polygon smaller than one nominal cell is explicitly returned as `POINT` mode at an interior representative point, rather than implying unsupported internal spatial variation.

Regional responses are deduplicated by returned cell center. Cell footprints are inferred halfway between the centers actually returned, intersected with the subject, and weighted by geodesic overlap area. The contract returns every cell series plus a daily weighted mean, weighted standard deviation, minimum, maximum and contributing-cell count. Missing cell-days are excluded and the available weights are renormalized for that date; no missing value becomes zero. The raw response is cached by request hash and retains `REAL_DATA`/`CACHED_DATA` provenance.

The live Regional endpoint returned HTTP 422 when asked for more than 366 days (`Please do not request a time period greater than 366 days`). K1 therefore partitions an inclusive long range by calendar year; each raw fragment has its own immutable cache key. Merging requires identical returned cell centers and units across fragments, rejects out-of-range dates and conflicting duplicates, and marks the combined source `REAL_DATA` when any fragment came from the provider (`CACHED_DATA` only when every fragment came from cache). A live check on 23-Oct-2026 requested 30-Dec-2023 through 2-Jan-2025 as three fragments (2 + 366 + 2 days) and returned 370 continuous daily aggregates for six intersecting cells using POWER API v2.10.1.

NASA's documentation describes regional output on a 0.5° × 0.5° grid and limits a regional request to one parameter. The live `T2M` response reflects the native MERRA-2 grid: **0.5° latitude × 0.625° longitude**. K1 therefore records the actual center spacing instead of hard-coding 0.5° in both axes.

Live checks on 23-Oct-2026, UTC daily data for 1–3 Jan 2026:

- Moquegua department (COD-AB): `REGIONAL`, 9 intersecting cells, 99.5833% inferred spatial coverage, spacing 0.5° × 0.625°, weighted T2M 13.0592 / 11.9289 / 13.5450 °C, with per-day dispersion and cell series retained.
- La Punta district, Callao (0.773019 km² simplified geometry): `POINT`, one representative point because the district is smaller than one nominal cell; T2M 19.18 / 19.02 / 19.03 °C.

These checks validate transport and aggregation, not local climate suitability. POWER remains a gridded product and does not replace station or site measurements.

## K3 real-estate pilot evidence

K3 accepts either user Polygon/MultiPolygon geometries or the pinned IGN/OCHA COD-AB Peru boundaries already catalogued above. IGN territories retain the source's CC BY-IGO attribution, 2015 creation date and the warning that they are administrative references rather than cadastral parcels. The versioned example uses three small polygons supplied as explicit `USER_ASSUMPTION` screening areas near Moquegua, Samegua and Cerro Colorado; they are neither client data nor claimed public parcels. Their ellipsoidal WGS84 areas are approximately 105,947, 105,958 and 106,407 m².

An IGN territory is accepted as an **area of analysis**, not as a development parcel. When any geometry exceeds 2 km² (200 ha), K3 keeps the NASA and territorial exposure evidence but adds a caution to that subject and to the fifth NASA answer: the conditional NPV applies the user's developable fraction and commercial assumptions to the whole geometry and therefore does not describe a project. Project evaluation requires a user-drawn or uploaded parcel polygon. With only one subject, `margin=null` and `tie=false` explicitly mean that no comparative ranking exists.

The 2018–2023 live run (`real-estate-screening-v1.0.example.json`, analysis `a9ae20e290ce6f69`) obtained cached official NASA POWER/K1 data and NASADEM `READY` for all three subjects. INGEMMET stayed `DATA_UNAVAILABLE`. P99 precipitation was 3.7564, 3.7564 and 3.6916 mm/day.
